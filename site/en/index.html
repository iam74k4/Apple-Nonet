Trackpad macro pad for Mac

Your trackpad becomes nine buttons.

Slide a finger down from the top edge and your MacBook trackpad turns into nine buttons that change with the app you are using. No key to hold: choose with one finger and lift to run. No extra device like a Stream Deck, and no shortcuts to memorize.

Slide in from the top edge, lift on a button

No key and no click. Put a finger on the top edge of the trackpad and slide it down to open the HUD, keep sliding to choose, and lift on a button to run it. The trackpad is split into a 3×3 grid of buttons with a frame around it.

Switch apps, and the nine buttons switch too

The nine things you use most in whatever app is in front. Set it up with presets and by picking from menus.

A profile for each app

Build and test in Xcode, mute and share your screen in Zoom. The nine buttons follow the app in front, and popular apps come with a ready-made layout.

Pick from the menus

Nonet reads the menus of a running app into a searchable list. Find the command and drag it onto an empty button. No need to look up shortcuts.

Three pages of nine, and a launcher

Hold your finger in the left or right frame to change pages, for up to 27 buttons. The Global pages start as a launcher filled with apps from your Mac, a handy stand-in for Launchpad.

A tick at every edge

On Force Touch trackpads, you feel a light tick each time your finger crosses into another button, so you can feel when you move to another button. Choose how strong it feels.

Scripts and AI

zsh scripts receive the front app, the open document, its project folder and the selected text as environment variables. On macOS 26, summarize or rewrite selected text with Apple Intelligence, right on your Mac.

FAQ

Can I get a refund?

Yes. Within 30 days of purchase, for any reason. Email us with your order number. The refund goes back to the payment method you used, through the store (see the terms).

How many Macs can I use it on?

One license covers every Mac you personally use. Each person needs their own license.

Keys are checked on your Mac, so there's nothing to activate or deactivate. On a new Mac, just enter the same license key.

Are updates free?

Yes. Every 1.x update is free. Nonet checks for new versions by itself, and you install them from its menu.

Could a macOS update stop Nonet from working?

It could. Nonet reads finger positions through a part of macOS that isn't public, so a major macOS update might break it. Each new macOS is tested from the first beta, and fixes ship as free updates. If Nonet stops working and can't be fixed, you get a refund no matter when you bought it.

What happens when the trial ends?

The buttons no longer open. Sliding in from the top edge shows "Your trial has ended" at the bottom of the screen instead. Your settings are kept, so entering a license key brings everything back immediately.

What are the requirements?

macOS 14 Sonoma or later and a multi-touch trackpad: the built-in trackpad of a MacBook or a Magic Trackpad. Both Apple silicon and Intel Macs are supported.

AI actions need macOS 26 or later on a Mac that supports Apple Intelligence.

Can I use it with a mouse only?

No. You open the buttons by sliding a finger in from the top edge of the trackpad and choose by where your finger is, so you need a trackpad. A Mac mini or Mac Studio works with a Magic Trackpad.

Which permissions does Nonet need?

Accessibility (System Settings › Privacy & Security). Nonet uses it to hold the pointer and keep clicks from reaching the app while the buttons are open, to notice a key press so it can close them, and to send keystrokes, choose menu items and read the selected text for actions. Reading finger positions needs no permission.

macOS asks once for Automation permission only when a script uses the browser's URL (NONET_BROWSER_URL). Running a script in Terminal doesn't ask.

Does Nonet send what I type or how I use it?

No. Nonet looks at the keyboard only while the nine buttons are open, and only to notice that a key was pressed so it can close them. It never records or sends what you type. Settings are stored only on your Mac (~/Library/Application Support/Nonet) and there is no account. The network is used only to check for updates and to open the purchase page.

See the privacy policy for details.

Will it open by accident while I use the trackpad as usual?

It opens only when one finger starts on the top edge of the trackpad and slides down. A move that starts more than 0.6 seconds after you touch, two or more fingers, a palm, a finger that moved into the edge from elsewhere, or a click-and-drag doesn't open it. Moving the pointer straight down from near the far edge can open it, so start moving the pointer away from the far edge.

For apps where you don't want it, choose Turn Off in This App in the Nonet menu. In those apps, sliding in from the top edge leaves the trackpad working as usual.

How do I close it without running anything?

Slide your finger out to the frame, the band around the nine buttons, and lift it. In the frame the buttons dim and “✕ Lift to cancel” appears. Pressing any key also closes them, and the key still reaches your app. A double tick tells you that you lifted in the frame and nothing ran.

Could I run something I can't undo, like Send, by mistake?

A button runs only if your finger pauses on it before you lift. Lifting while you slide past doesn't run it.

For buttons you can't undo, such as Send or Record, turn on Hold before running in the button's settings. It then runs only if you hold your finger on it for about 0.45 s, until its outline in the HUD has gone all the way around (the trackpad ticks), and then lift.

Will it get in the way of typing or keyboard shortcuts?

No. Opening the buttons uses only the trackpad, never a key. Nonet looks at the keyboard only while the nine buttons are open: pressing a key then closes them without running anything, and the key reaches your app as usual.

Shortcuts you record for a button work the same on JIS, US and other layouts.

Why isn't it on the Mac App Store?

To read finger positions while you use other apps, Nonet relies on a part of macOS that is not public. The App Store does not allow that, so Nonet is sold directly from this site. The app is signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple.

How do I uninstall Nonet?

Quit Nonet, move it from the Applications folder to the Trash, delete its settings files and the trial record in your keychain, and remove it from the Accessibility list. The user guide has the exact steps.

How is this different from a Stream Deck?

Stream Deck is a dedicated device from Elgato with a grid of keys that each have their own display. Nonet is software that uses your MacBook trackpad as the buttons. You need no extra hardware, but there are no physical keys or per-key screens, and the trackpad becomes buttons only when you slide in from the top edge to open them.
